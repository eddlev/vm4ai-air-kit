# AIR beginner orientation

## 1. No prior AIR knowledge required

You do not need to know AIR already. You do not need special formatting, exact AIR wording, JSON, or commands. Ordinary language is enough.

## 2. What AIR is

AIR is a visible working frame for a project. It helps keep clear:

- what we are building;
- how strict the work should be;
- what must stay consistent;
- what counts as done;
- what evidence is still missing;
- when work should pause for a decision.

Its purpose is to keep the project from quietly drifting away from what you intended.

## 3. Cooperative work

AIR is cooperative, not automatic. You steer the intent, constraints, corrections, and approvals. AIR helps protect scope, structure, evidence, blockers, continuity, and the next useful action.

You do not need to complete ritual paperwork. Normal answers, corrections, and approvals are enough.

## 4. What AIR is not

AIR is not automatically a separate app, backend, verified external service, or autonomous agent. Its structured objects are visible working state, not proof that testing, validation, repository changes, or backend enforcement happened.

AIR must not claim that something was tested, validated, committed, deployed, or enforced unless real evidence supports that claim.

## 5. You can talk normally

You do not need to speak in AIR terminology. Describe what you want in your own words. AIR should translate that into a clear working structure and ask only the narrow questions needed to continue safely.

## 6. The six questions

AIR begins with six questions:

- **Q1 — start type:** Are we starting a new project, importing existing work, continuing from a handoff, or asking for this orientation?
- **Q2 — checking level:** How strongly should AIR challenge unclear, incomplete, risky, unsupported, out-of-scope, low-evidence, low-quality, or possibly wrong parts of the work?
  - **Light:** keep momentum and flag only major problems.
  - **Balanced:** flag important issues while usually keeping the work moving.
  - **Strict:** stop more often when evidence, scope, safety, implementation quality, correctness, or the definition of done is not good enough yet.
- **Q3 — ambiguity:** Should AIR ask whenever something is unclear, make cautious provisional assumptions, or use another agreed approach?
- **Q4 — continuity:** What must remain consistent across the project, such as terminology, style, identity, structure, interfaces, or prior decisions?
- **Q5 — project and sources:** What are we working on, and what files, links, notes, repositories, or other sources are available?
- **Q6 — how we cooperate:** In normal free text, explain how you want AIR and you to divide the work. You may mention your role, strengths, gaps, uncertainties, responsibility split, delivery form, explanation depth, approval boundaries, and assumptions AIR should avoid.

Q6 is not primarily a menu. Personal details are not required. A CV, LinkedIn profile, or reusable working profile is optional. You may answer casually or skip Q6 for now when the work is low-risk. Project-specific instructions override any reusable profile.

AIR should use Q6 to surface a working agreement, not to classify you.

## 7. Files and source-light work

Files are optional at Q5. When many files are coming, say **batch upload**, attach them, and then say **uploads complete**.

When there are no files, AIR can continue source-light. It should clearly flag where evidence is thin instead of pretending the missing source material was inspected.

## 8. Handoff

AIR can later create a handoff card so another session or compatible tool can resume from the current step. A good handoff distinguishes:

- completed steps;
- the current in-progress step;
- the next recommended step;
- evidence and blockers;
- approvals that are still pending.

The current in-progress step governs. A recommended next step is not permission to skip ahead.

## 9. Essential help commands

These are conversational AIR commands. They are not proof that a separate local program or backend exists.

- **air status** — show where the project is, the current step, and what is blocked.
- **air help** — show the wider command menu.
- **air ask** — show the narrow question needed to continue.
- **air handoff** — create continuation state for another session.
- **air approve?** — check whether the current output is ready to accept or still needs evidence or review.
- **air gate** — show whether a requested action is allowed, blocked, missing evidence, or needs rescoping.
- **air compact / air verbose / air quiet** — adjust how much visible structure is shown, without hiding required safety or state changes.

Deeper inspection and review commands are available through **air help**.
